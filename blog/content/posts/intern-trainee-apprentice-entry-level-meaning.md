The same first job can be advertised as an internship, a traineeship, an apprenticeship or an entry-level role, and two postings with the same title can mean different things. InternDoor reads every posting it lists, so we counted the words that appear in the titles: 915 engineering postings we filed as internships between 1 August and 12 October 2026, and 1,664 entry-level full-time jobs between 18 September and 12 October 2026, all in India.

## Four words, four rough meanings

::: chart titles.intern

### Intern

"Intern" or "internship" was in 645 of the 915 internship titles. An internship is a fixed-length placement, usually for students or very recent graduates, and usually with an end date rather than a promise of a job. In the one month where we checked every stated figure by hand, all ten roles that stated a stipend were six months long. Whether an internship can convert into a full-time job is up to the employer, and the posting may or may not say.

### Trainee

"Trainee" was in 142 internship titles, and it covers two different things. Sometimes it is a training programme for students or fresh graduates that the employer treats as an internship. Sometimes, as in many "Graduate Engineer Trainee" roles, it is the first grade of a full-time job, where the training period is the start of employment. The title alone does not tell you which, and the postings rarely help: of the 142 trainee postings, only 27 stated how long the role lasts.

### Apprentice

"Apprentice" was in 119 internship titles. In India, apprenticeship is also a government scheme. Under the National Apprenticeship Training Scheme, the government set a stipend of ₹9,000 a month for graduate apprentices in engineering and other streams, and ₹8,000 for diploma holders, for 2021-22 to 2025-26, and funded stipend support for apprentices trained by employers.[^nats-2021] In May 2025 the Central Apprenticeship Council recommended raising apprentice stipends by 36%.[^cac-2025] Check the scheme's current rate before relying on either figure.

An "apprentice" title on a company's own posting may mean a place under that scheme, or it may simply be the name the company gives its training roles. Ask which. The terms, the stipend and what you can claim at the end can all differ.

### Entry level, associate, level I

Full-time jobs for new graduates rarely announce themselves. Of the 1,664 entry-level full-time jobs InternDoor listed in India over those four weeks, 95 had "associate" in the title and 101 a level such as "Engineer I" or "SDE-1". Most had neither: the title was simply the job.

And a job board's "entry level" label is not a promise. Over a month of India searches filtered to entry-level roles, InternDoor turned away thousands of postings that the label had let through: 6,119 with a senior, lead or principal title, and 1,539 whose own text asked for two or more years of experience.

::: chart refused.entry

## Read these four lines instead of the title

Whatever the title says, four lines in the body of the posting tell you what the role is.

1. **The length.** A start date and an end date, or a number of months. If neither is there, the role may be open-ended employment rather than a fixed placement, or the employer may not have decided. Ask.
2. **The pay.** Only 4% of September's engineering internship postings stated a stipend, so you will usually have to ask. Our [report on stipends](/internship-postings-stipend-disclosure) lists what the ones that did state a figure offered.
3. **The experience asked for.** Read the requirement line, not the label on the search filter. "Entry level" with "3+ years required" is a three-year role.
4. **Who it is for.** A graduation year, a batch, a degree or a branch. A posting for the 2026 batch is not open to the 2028 batch, whatever its title says.

Two more lines matter after an offer: whether the role can convert into a full-time job, and whether that conversion comes with a bond or a service agreement. One September internship posting stated a two-year service agreement with a ₹2 lakh bond for interns who converted.

## A quick reference

Table: What each word usually signals, and what to check
| Word in the title | Usually means | Check before you apply |
|---|---|---|
| Intern, internship | A fixed-length placement for students or recent graduates | Length, stipend, whether it can convert |
| Trainee | A training programme, or the first grade of a full-time job | Whether it is employment, how long the training lasts, any bond |
| Apprentice | A company training role, or a place under the government apprenticeship scheme | Which of the two it is, and the stipend |
| Associate, Engineer I, SDE-1 | A full-time entry-level grade | The years of experience asked for |

::: method
The titles are from engineering postings InternDoor recorded in India from employers on its list: 915 internships first seen between 1 August and 12 October 2026, and 1,664 entry-level full-time jobs first seen between 18 September, when InternDoor began listing them, and 12 October 2026. A word counts when it appears in the title as a whole word, so "internal" is not "intern"; one title can carry several of the words. The refused counts are India postings returned for entry-level searches between 13 September and 12 October 2026 that InternDoor did not list, counted once per posting and city.
