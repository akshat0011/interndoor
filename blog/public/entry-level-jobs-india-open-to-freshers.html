Only 1 in 10 “Entry level” engineering jobs in India is open to a fresher

Freshers filter job boards by “Entry level” and trust the label. We checked every engineering posting that label returned in India for three weeks, from the employers on our list, and counted how many a new graduate could actually apply to.

Key findings

  1. 10% of 20,118 engineering postings tagged Entry level were open to a fresher: 2,054 postings, between 25 September and 13 October 2026.
  2. 44% had senior, lead, principal or staff in the title. Another 7% were level II or higher, 6% were manager roles and 6% consultant or architect roles.
  3. 18% looked like junior jobs by their title but asked for two or more years in their own text. 1,280 of those asked for five years or more.
  4. 62% could be ruled out from the title alone, before reading a word of the posting.

A new graduate looking for a first engineering job usually does the obvious thing: opens a job board, sets the experience filter to “Entry level”, and starts applying. The filter is meant to do the sorting. We wanted to know how well it does.

For three weeks, InternDoor ran that search for engineering roles in India about once an hour and checked every posting it returned against the rules our board uses for entry-level jobs. We counted only postings from the roughly 1,900 employers on our list, so the results are about established companies, not anonymous recruiters.

What the label let through

Of 20,118 postings tagged Entry level, 2,054 passed every check. The other nine in ten were not jobs a fresher could reasonably apply to.

Most failed on the title. These are real titles from postings that carried the Entry level tag:

A title like “Software Development Engineer II” is not a mistake by the employer. Level II is usually the second rung, for people with a few years behind them. The tag on the posting says something different.

The ones that look junior and are not

The harder cases are the 3,660 postings whose title gave nothing away. “Full Stack Developer - Python”, “Application Analyst”, “Gen AI developer” and “Data Engineer” all read like first jobs. Their own descriptions asked for two, three, four or five years or more.

More than a third of them asked for five years or more. A further 1,176 postings had no warning in the title or the requirements we could read, but the posting itself gave its level as Mid-Senior.

What to do if you are applying

This is advice, from the patterns above.

  1. Search with the words employers use for first jobs, not only the filter: “fresher”, “graduate engineer trainee”, “associate software engineer”, “SDE 1”, “2026 batch” or “2027 batch”.
  2. Skip titles with II, III, Senior, Lead, Staff, Principal or Manager in them, whatever the tag says. That alone removes most of what does not fit.
  3. Find the experience line before you apply. The number of years is usually in the requirements or qualifications section. “0 to 2 years” is open to you; “3 to 5 years” is not, whatever label it carries.
  4. Do not count the filter as a measure of the market. A search that returns 300 entry-level roles may hold 30 you can apply to. That is a reason to apply early to those 30, not a reason to give up.

For placement cells and colleges

When students say there are plenty of entry-level openings on job boards but no interview calls, part of the answer is in these numbers: most of what the filter shows them was never meant for them. Teaching students to read the title and the experience line takes a few minutes and saves dozens of wasted applications.

If you use these figures, please cite them as: InternDoor, “Only 1 in 10 Entry level engineering jobs in India is open to a fresher”, 17 October 2026, with a link to this page.

How this was counted

The postings are every engineering posting in India that InternDoor’s hourly entry-level search was shown between 25 September and 13 October 2026, from 439 runs of that search, filtered to the Entry level experience tag alone. From 14 October the search also included the Associate tag, so later runs are left out. The search returned 59,479 different postings; 37,470 came from employers that are not on InternDoor’s list and 1,882 were not engineering roles, which leaves the 20,118 counted here. Each posting is counted once, however many cities or runs it appeared in, and in the first check it failed: the title first, then the posting’s own text. “Asked for two or more years” uses the smallest figure a posting stated, so “3 to 5 years” counts as three. “Other reasons” (739) are postings that gave a different level on the posting itself (432, mostly Associate, which InternDoor now lists), were contract, part-time or temporary (172), were turned away by an earlier rule of ours that has since been corrected (128), or could not be read (6), plus one postdoc role. Those 128 may have been open to a fresher, so the true share could be up to 0.6 points higher. “Open to a fresher” means the posting passed every check when InternDoor read it; a few were held back later, when a fuller reading of the text found two or more years. An earlier post counted only the postings turned away, over a different month and once per posting and city, so its figures are smaller than these. Full definitions are on How we count.
