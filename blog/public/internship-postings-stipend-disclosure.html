Only 4% of engineering internship postings in India say what they pay

Postings tell you where you will sit far more often than what you will be paid. We read every September posting that named a figure and list them here, with what to ask when a posting says nothing.

Key findings

  1. 4% of September’s 425 engineering internship postings stated a stipend: 18 postings from 10 roles. In August the share was 7%, 23 of 333.1
  2. 22% said how long the internship lasts.
  3. 82% said whether the work is on site, hybrid or remote.
  4. None of the ten busiest employers stated a stipend on any posting, in either month.2

Two of the first things an applicant needs to know about an internship are what it pays and how long it lasts. They are also the two things a posting is least likely to say. We went through all 425 engineering internships posted in India in September 2026 and recorded which facts each one actually stated.

What the postings said, and what they left out

The pattern is consistent. Facts about the job itself are usually there: where it is, how the work is done, which tools it uses. Money and time are usually not. Fewer than one posting in four said how long the internship would last, and fewer than one in twenty said what it would pay.

A posting that states no stipend is not necessarily unpaid. It means the figure was not on the listing. Every employer in these counts is on InternDoor’s list because we believe it is a real company that pays its interns.

A few things stand out from a small list.

  • Seven of the ten gave a monthly figure, between ₹10,000 and ₹45,000. At least four of those seven were ₹20,000 a month or less.
  • Three stated a figure without saying per month. That is probably a monthly figure, but a posting that does not say so has not said it.
  • Two separated fixed pay from the rest. One paid ₹16,000 of its ₹20,000 as a fixed amount; another paid ₹8,000 fixed and up to ₹4,000 variable.
  • All ten were six months long, and all ten said so.
  • Two also stated what a full-time offer would pay after the internship: up to ₹3 LPA in one case and ₹5 LPA in the other. The second also stated a two-year service agreement with a ₹2 lakh bond on conversion.

This is not a picture of what engineering interns in India are paid. The ten roles are the ones whose employers chose to state a figure, which is a small and self-selected group. Treat the list as a calibration point, not a market rate.

A correction to our own count

Our frozen September report counted 19 postings with a stipend. When we checked each one by hand for this post, one turned out to be a figure read from a sentence about the year the company was founded. The correct count is 18. The percentage does not change, and the frozen report stays as it was compiled, so this post is where the correction lives.

What to ask when a posting is silent

Asking about pay is an ordinary part of applying, and it is better asked in writing, before the final round, than discovered on the offer letter.

  • The stipend, and whether it is monthly. “Could you share the monthly stipend and the length of this internship?” is enough.
  • What is fixed and what is variable. Two of the ten roles above split the figure. A variable part is not a guaranteed part.
  • What happens after. Whether the internship can convert to a full-time job, what that job would pay, and whether a conversion comes with a bond or a service agreement.
  • Whether anything is deducted or charged. A genuine employer does not charge you to work for it. Our guide to checking a posting covers the warning signs.

How this was counted

The 425 postings are every engineering internship InternDoor recorded in India in September 2026, from the monthly report. A stipend counts as stated when the posting gives a figure for the intern’s pay; each of the 18 was read against the posting’s own text in October 2026. A length counts as stated when the posting gives the internship’s duration; an experience range such as “0 to 1 years” does not count. Work arrangement and skills are read from the posting text. The August figure is from the frozen August report and was not re-checked by hand. Full definitions are on How we count.
